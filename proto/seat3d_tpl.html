What 3D actually fixes

The shipped game is 3D pretending on a 2D canvas: every seat is a sprite baked ahead of time by art/seat_atlas.py, one per colour per facing. Three bugs came straight out of that, and none of them can happen here:

  • Seats faced the wall. Facings 1 and 3 were swapped in the bake table, so side-on seats had their backs to the aisle and passengers hopped in over the backrest. Here a facing is a rotation — there is nothing to get backwards.
  • Height was crushed four to one. In the flat projection one unit across drew 28.7px but one unit of height drew 6.9px, so anything modelled with sane numbers came out squashed. Here height is height.
  • New furniture never matched. The obstacle table took three attempts because it had to be judged against lit sprites it was not baked with. Here everything sits under one light.
  • Landscape is free. Try View: landscape. Nothing about the board changes — the room turns. The 2D prototype had to transpose the data to escape portrait, which mirrors every level so a board you know stops looking like itself. Here it is a camera move, and the puzzle is untouched.

The art pass that followed: every solid is a rounded rectangle extruded with a bevel rather than a box, each colour carries three tones instead of one flat fill, and passengers wear the deep tone so they do not vanish into the seat they match. The room got a plinth, walls with lit windows, a handrail and a door frame — the details that make it a place rather than a tray.

A full seat stays on the floor here, because that is what the shipped engine does — it stops taking passengers and goes on being furniture. An earlier build of this prototype flew them away, copied from bench_rush.html, an abandoned prototype that the solver script still points at. Nothing in the real engine removes a seat.

What it costs

  • Payload. Three.js is about 600KB from the CDN — the bundle goes from 4.6MB to roughly 5.2MB, still far under the 20MB that keeps the mobile front page.
  • Time to gameplay. CrazyGames grades this, and it is measured to the gameplayStart call. A 600KB library must not sit on the boot path — it loads when a level opens, the same trick already used for the analytics script.
  • Art direction. Rough 3D looks worse than careful 2D. One light rig and one palette, fixed early and not touched.

Measured on level 19, the biggest board here: 438 draw calls and 142k triangles. The triangles are fine; the draw calls are not, because this prototype builds every seat from eight separate meshes. Shipping it means one instanced call per colour, which takes 438 down to about a dozen. Boot to first frame is 1.0s off local disk.

Boards are the real campaign, in their original orientation — not transposed the way the honey prototype does it. The rules come from the same shared file both prototypes read, so what is legal here is what is legal in the shipped game.
